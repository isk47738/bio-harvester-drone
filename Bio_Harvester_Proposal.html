National Engineering Innovation Initiative

PROJECT JAL-SHODHAK (BIO-HARVESTER)

Autonomous Amphibious Waterway Restoration Drone with In-Situ Biomass-to-Biofuel Extrusion
Discipline Mechanical & Mechatronics
Focus Area Canal Desiltation & Irrigation
Core Innovation Twin-Catamaran Extrusion Drone
Output Clean Canals + Biofuel Pellets
National Mission Statement: To safeguard our nation's agrarian arteries by deploying autonomous amphibious robotic harvesters that clear weed-choked irrigation canals, restore natural drainage to prevent catastrophic monsoon floods, and convert noxious aquatic biomass into decentralized clean biofuel for rural communities.

1 The National Crisis & Problem Statement

Millions of kilometers of vital irrigation canals, drainage ditches, and river networks suffer from severe biological and hydrological choke points that threaten national food security and public safety:

Core Structural Threats:
  • Invasive Weed Infestation: Water Hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes) doubles every 12 days, cutting canal water flow rate by up to 70% and suffocating aquatic ecology.
  • Agrarian Deprivation & Monsoon Floods: Choked canals prevent irrigation waters from reaching tail-end farming communities during critical sowing periods, while causing violent embankment breaches and flash floods during monsoons.
  • Hazardous Conventional Remediation: Heavy diesel earthmovers (JCBs) operate from banks, fracturing earthen lining, burning ~14 L/hr of diesel, and dumping rotting piles on roadsides that generate foul odor, disease vectors, and methane emissions.
🚨 Conventional Backhoe Dredging

Destroys canal earthen bunds, consumes massive diesel, leaves sub-surface roots intact, and creates decomposing roadside waste heaps.

💡 The Jal-Shodhak Solution

Ultralight amphibious craft operating in-channel. Solar-electric powered, cuts roots 0.8m deep, shreds and converts wet weed into solid biofuel pellets on-board.

3 Mechanical & Mechatronic Engineering Subsystems

A. Amphibious Twin-Catamaran Hull & Crawlers

Built from marine-grade corrosion-resistant Aluminum 5083-H116 and rotomolded HDPE floats. Features an ultra-shallow operational draft of 0.22 meters to glide effortlessly over shallow weed beds and mud shoals.

  • Propulsion: Dual 48V steerable BLDC Kort-nozzle thrusters with weed-chopping hub blades to prevent fouling.
  • Bank Traversal: Retractable paddle-crawler tracks allow the drone to climb across canal bunds, locks, and weirs.
B. Submerged Harvester & Mulcher

Front articulated conveyor table equipped with dual reciprocating cutter bars engineered with tungsten-carbide serrated teeth. Submerges up to 0.9 m to cleanly slice root crowns.

  • Hexagonal Shredder: High-torque twin shafts running at 180 RPM shred dense vegetation into 15–20 mm uniform mulch.
  • Anti-Jam Torque Monitor: Dynamic load sensing automatically reverses cutters if nylon rope or scrap debris is detected.
C. Continuous Screw-Press Dewaterer

Water hyacinth contains 92–95% water by weight. Jal-Shodhak uses a progressive tapered compression screw with a slotted wedge-wire cylindrical screen.

  • Moisture Drop: Reduces moisture from 94% down to under 35% in seconds.
  • Clean Effluent Return: Filtered, oxygenated water returns to the canal, replenishing local dissolved oxygen.
D. Radial Die Extrusion Pelletizer

The compressed biomass cake enters a rotary roller die extruder. Motor cooling jacket heat (85°C) liquefies the plant's natural lignin, bonding dense 8mm solid fuel pellets without chemical additives.

  • Energy Density: ~16.8 MJ/kg (competes directly with dry wood briquettes).
  • On-Board Bunker: 350 kg capacity with automated offloading auger for seamless canal-bank unloading.

7 12-Month Prototype & National Deployment Roadmap

Quarter 1: CAD & Lab Bench Prototyping
  • Hydrodynamic simulation (ANSYS Fluent) of catamaran hull under weed drag.
  • Fabrication of scale screw-press dewaterer; torque and dewatering test bench.
  • Dataset collection and training of YOLOv8 weed-segmentation model.
Quarter 2: Full-Scale Fabrication
  • Welding and CNC fabrication of Al-5083 catamaran hull and conveyor structure.
  • Integration of 48V powertrain, solar canopy, and LiFePO4 battery management.
  • Static buoyancy, balance, and emergency bilge pump validation in tank test.
Quarter 3: Irrigation Canal Field Trials
  • Deployment in a 5 km heavily choked agricultural canal pilot stretch.
  • Validation of automated RTK-GNSS waypoint navigation and collision avoidance.
  • Chemical calorific testing (bomb calorimeter) of extruded biofuel pellets.
Quarter 4: Autonomous Fleet Swarm & Handover
  • Implementation of multi-drone swarm coordination for broad lake clearing.
  • Creation of open-source maintenance manual and training for rural youth cooperatives.
  • Formal presentation to National Irrigation and Water Resources Ministries.

8 Direct National Impact & Sovereign Benefits

🌾 Food Security & Irrigation Flow

Guarantees full designed hydraulic flow through canal networks, ensuring vital irrigation water reaches vulnerable downstream smallholder farmers on schedule during dry sowing seasons.

🛡️ Flood Mitigation & Disaster Resilience

Pre-monsoon canal desiltation and weed clearing prevents backflow surges and structural embankment breaches that cause catastrophic loss of life and rural livestock annually.

⚡ Rural Clean Fuel Independence

Supplies women and rural households with clean, smokeless biomass pellets, replacing dangerous indoor firewood burning and reducing acute respiratory illnesses.

🇮🇳 Atmanirbhar Technological Sovereignty

Replaces multi-crore imported diesel dredger boats with indigenous, open mechatronic technology built, serviced, and operated locally by national engineering talent.

Conclusion & Call to Action: Project Jal-Shodhak demonstrates how mechanical engineering, robotics, and circular economy design can directly solve one of the nation's most persistent ecological and agrarian crises. By turning an environmental hazard into a source of clean energy and water security, this project serves both the land and its people.
